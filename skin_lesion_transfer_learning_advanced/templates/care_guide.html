This guide provides general information, not personalized treatment or a diagnosis.

Emergency warning

Seek emergency help for a rash accompanied by difficulty breathing, swelling of lips/tongue/throat, faintness, or rapidly worsening severe symptoms. Get prompt medical advice for fever with a rash, severe pain, blistering, involvement of eyes/mouth, or a rapidly spreading rash.

LOW-RISK GENERAL CARE

What may help protect skin

  • Use a gentle, fragrance-free cleanser and avoid scrubbing irritated skin.
  • For ordinary dryness, a simple fragrance-free moisturizer may help; stop if it causes irritation.
  • Avoid suspected irritants and keep notes about new products or exposures.
  • Do not apply household chemicals, unlabelled mixtures or someone else's prescription medicine.
  • Ask a qualified healthcare professional or pharmacist before using medicated creams or tablets, especially for children, pregnancy, allergies or other medicines.

Why this app does not prescribe creams or tablets

A similar-looking rash can have different causes, including irritation, eczema, infection or allergic reactions. The wrong cream can make some conditions worse. A photo classifier cannot safely select a prescription or confirm the cause.

Prepare for a clinician visit

  • Record when the rash started and whether it is changing.
  • List new skincare products, detergents, medicines or possible exposures.
  • Bring your photo timeline if you choose to keep one, but do not delay care to collect photos.
Important

This project is a student research prototype. It does not provide a diagnosis, treatment plan or medication recommendation. For a persistent or concerning skin problem, contact a qualified clinician.
